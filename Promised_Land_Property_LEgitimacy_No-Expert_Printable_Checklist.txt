Phase 1: 5-Minute Public Records Checks (No Group Help Needed)

✅ ‌Step 1:‌ Visit your local county natural resources bureau, show your ID, and request the full official title deed for this property.

Confirm the legal registered owner is the formally registered religious/community group, not the leader’s personal name
Confirm no court disputes, unpaid mortgages, or expropriation notices are listed on the file
Confirm the land use type (residential/religious/agricultural) matches what the group claims
If any of these fail: STOP. The claim is fraudulent.‌

✅ ‌Step 2:‌ Check the group’s official religious registration certificate at the local religious affairs bureau.

Confirm the group holds a valid, active Religious Activity Site Registration Certificate tied to this exact property
Confirm the property is not listed as a private individual’s personal asset
If no valid certificate exists: STOP. The group has no legal right to operate here.‌

✅ ‌Step 3:‌ Pull the latest "房地一体" (house + land integrated)确权 record from the local land registry.

Confirm the property boundaries and total area exactly match what the group is selling/assigning to members
Confirm no part of the claimed "promised land" overlaps with public roads, protected forests, or neighboring private land
If boundaries do not match: STOP. The group is selling land they do not legally own.‌
Phase 2: Simple On-Site & Local Checks

✅ ‌Step 4:‌ Walk the full property boundary with a free local village committee staff member.

Ask them to confirm no part of the land is public village collective property that cannot be privately sold
Ask 2+ long-term local residents (not group members) if the group’s claimed "ancient/ancestral covenant" history matches local memory
If locals say the group only moved in within the last 3 years and made up the history: Flag as high risk.‌

✅ ‌Step 5:‌ Check all buildings on the property for posted permits.

Look for visible, official construction permits and fire safety inspection stickers on every occupied building
Confirm no buildings were built on agricultural land without government conversion approval
If no permits are posted: The buildings are illegal and can be demolished at any time.‌

✅ ‌Step 6:‌ Ask the group for a full list of all members who have paid for a "personal plot" on this land.

Cross-check every name on this list against the official title deed: none of these members will appear as legal co-owners in a scam
If zero paying members are listed on the official title: This is a classic divine title fraud scam.‌
Phase 3: Automatic Red Flags (Any 1 = 100% Fraud)

Check every box that applies:
⬜ The group forbids you from visiting the local land bureau or checking public records, calling this "betrayal of the faith"
⬜ The group demands you sign over all your personal savings/assets to the leader in exchange for a "spiritual plot"
⬜ The group claims "national land laws do not apply here" and says you can legally occupy adjacent private land
⬜ The group only gives you a self-printed "divine covenant deed" instead of a government-issued property certificate
⬜ The group cannot show you a single member who has successfully resold or legally inherited their "promised land" plot

Final Simple Score
0 red flags, all Phase 1 checks passed: ‌Legitimate low-risk claim‌
1-2 red flags, partial records missing: ‌High risk, do not invest any money or move in‌
3+ red flags: ‌Confirmed scam. Report to local natural resources and public security departments immediately‌

You do not need a lawyer to run these checks. Every single public record request is free or costs less than 10 USD in most regions.
